What I mean by useful

A small test for AI agents: do they make real work clearer, easier to review and easier to move forward?

Useful is a demanding word. A system can look capable in a demonstration and still add very little to an ordinary working day.

I am more interested in what happens on a Tuesday morning: someone has a supplier decision to make, three incomplete documents, and an approval that cannot wait. An agent helps when it can take on a clear piece of that work, show what it did, and make it easy for a person to correct or stop it.

That changes how I think about product design. I favour quiet reliability over spectacle, reversible actions over vague autonomy, and tools that fit the work people already have.

The point is not to remove people from the process. It is to give them more room for the exceptions, trade-offs and conversations that actually need them.

That is the standard I want to keep applying: less friction around the work, more clarity around the decision, and control that remains with the person responsible.

Max Ducroisy · London
